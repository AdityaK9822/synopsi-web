HIST231 — European History 1750–1914, Lecture 6 — THE INDUSTRIAL REVOLUTION (I): BRITAIN 1760–1850

1. why Britain first?
   - coal + iron + navigable rivers + capital from trade. Politically: no internal tolls post-Union, Enclosure Act (1773-ish) (displaces peasants into the factory towns).
   - institutional: London's flexible capital market + patent office.
   FIRST factories into the Midlands; population shifts Manchester, Birmingham, Liverpool.

2. Enclosure movement (1760–1820)
   - open-field strips -> fenced, private plots. Agricultural yield rose (selective breeding — "Turnip" Townsend vs Jethro Tull drill? prof: drill credited to Tull, extoll 1701-ish)
   - social cost: rural surplus labour -> towns; he mentions "downwards mobility" and emergence of rural dependency; a *commons-loss* that Marx later critiqued.

3. 1769 — James Watt patents condenser; 1792 first rotary steam engine. Watts manufacturing agreement with Boulton (Lunar Society) — reliable power led to factory concentration — proximity to raw materials decreased because water wheels no longer constrained.
4. Textiles specifics
   - flying shuttle (Kay 1733) → SPEED; spinning jenny (Hargreaves 1764 — multithread); water frame (Arkwright 1769); Crompton's mule (1779) combines strength+fine thread. 1787 power loom by Edmund Cartwright ("power loom") — but steam loom not reliable until 1800s.
   - "factory system" begins here — capitalist, concentrated, disciplined labor. Francis Place + later utilitarian urban redesign.
   - textiles = first sector to exploit steam + division of labour.
   - Arkwright tycoon status.

5. transportation
   - 1804: Trevithick's steam locomotive; 1825: Stockton-Darlington (George Stephenson); 1830 Liverpool-Manchester (first public passenger rail). rail network booms -> removes local monopolies on goods, enables more unified markets. Canals (Napoleonic-era) gave phys constraint & damage.
6. capitalism & factory discipline
   - Boulton/Watt (proto-intellectual capitalism); Ure/Hookland manufacturing arguments ("Mill nineteenth century").
   - Corporation legislated as infrastructure builder (railway company). Limited liability (initially for transport) gave modern corporate form.
